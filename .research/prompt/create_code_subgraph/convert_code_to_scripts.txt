# Experimental Design
- Strategy: Title: One-Shot Pareto Benchmark of HYPE-BABS on a Single NVIDIA A100

Goal
Quantitatively prove that HYPE-BABS delivers a uniformly better Accuracy–Latency–Energy trade-off than state-of-the-art neighbour samplers on realistic, large-scale GNN training.  

Rationale
• The A100 has ample memory (80 GB) to host all candidate samplers simultaneously, eliminating I/O variance.  
• A single, carefully controlled hardware setting isolates the sampler’s contribution from inter-device noise.  
• A unified Pareto evaluation condenses three objectives into one visual, decisive verdict.  

Experimental Steps
1. Datasets (hosted in RAM → zero loading jitter)
   a. OGB-Products (large, homophilic)
   b. Chameleon (medium, heterophilic)
   c. OGB-Arxiv-DGL (40-layer stress test)

2. Model
   16-layer GATv2, hidden dim 128, ELU, dropout 0.2.  Identical initialisation across runs (torch.manual_seed 42).  

3. Samplers
   Full-batch, GraphSAINT-Edge, Bandit-BS.M, LBS, BLABS, HYPE-BABS (ours).  Hyper-parameters copied verbatim from their papers; HYPE-BABS uses default α = 0.5, β = 0.02, K₀ ∈ {64,128,256}.  

4. Measurement pipeline
   • Wrap the training loop in a Python context manager that every 100 ms records:
     – Wall-clock time (time.perf_counter).
     – Instantaneous board power draw via nvidia-smi --query-gpu=power.draw.
   • Integrate power over time to obtain energy (Δe).  
   • Log train / validation loss, accuracy, global budget K_t, and per-layer variance.

5. Training protocol
   • 500 epochs, AdamW (lr 3e-4, weight-decay 1e-2).  
   • Early stop if validation accuracy saturates for 30 epochs.  
   • Three seeds per setting to report mean±σ.  

6. Evaluation metrics
   a. Final test accuracy.
   b. Mean epoch latency (Δt).
   c. Mean epoch energy (Δe).
   d. Area under Pareto Front (AUPF): integrate convex hull in (accuracy, latency) and (accuracy, energy) planes.  Larger = better.

7. Analysis
   • Plot Pareto fronts of all samplers.  HYPE-BABS must lie strictly on the upper-left (better) boundary.  
   • Overlay budget-annealing curves K_t to visualise adaptive shrinking.  
   • Histogram node-wise k after epoch 400 to show heavy-tailed distribution.  
   • Ablations (α = 1, no annealing, one-level bandit) run only on OGB-Products to isolate each component’s impact.  

Expected Outcomes and What They Demonstrate
1. HYPE-BABS reaches within ±0.2 % of the highest accuracy among all samplers while reducing median epoch time by ≈35 % and energy by ≈40 % compared with BLABS.  ⇒ Confirms P1 & P2: fine-grained, latency-aware budgeting lowers hardware cost without hurting performance.
2. Its Pareto front dominates: AUPF improves by ≥15 % over the second-best method on every dataset.  ⇒ Shows multi-objective optimality, not just one metric.
3. Annealing curve: global edge budget K_t falls to <25 % of K₀ after 300 epochs, yet accuracy plateaus, unlike baselines whose K stays flat.  ⇒ Verifies P3: adaptive budget shrinkage removes wasted computation.
4. Node-wise k histogram: 70 % of low-degree nodes end with k = 1, while top 5 % hubs retain k ≥ 8.  Variance heat-map confirms smoother per-layer gradients.  ⇒ Validates node×layer granularity (addresses P1).
5. Ablations: removing latency term raises energy by ~25 %; disabling annealing increases edges processed by ~18 % with no accuracy gain; discarding budget-bandit drops accuracy −2 %.  ⇒ Each design choice is necessary.

Conclusion
Executing this single, tightly controlled A100 experiment yields decisive evidence that HYPE-BABS simultaneously minimises training time and energy for a given accuracy, fulfilling the three open problems left by BLABS.  The strategy leverages the powerful hardware to eliminate confounders and presents results in an industry-relevant Pareto framework that stakeholders can immediately interpret.

EXPERIMENT : One-Shot Pareto Benchmark of HYPE-BABS on a Single NVIDIA A100
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. MODELS
   1. Primary architecture – GATv2-16
      • 16 layers, hidden-dim 128, ELU, dropout 0.2, residual connections every 2 layers, layer-norm.
      • #Heads = 4 for products/chameleon, 8 for arxiv (40-layer graph is deeper, we keep head-count modest to fit A100 cache).
   2. Baseline architecture – same GATv2-16 BUT trained with alternative neighbour samplers below.  Model weights, optimiser states and LR schedulers are re-initialised for every run with identical seed to guarantee fair comparison.
   (Reason: isolates the *sampler* as the only degree of freedom.)

B. DATASETS
   1. OGB-Products  (2.4 M nodes, 123 M edges, 100 classes)
   2. Chameleon      (2 k nodes,   59 k edges, 5  classes) – heterophilic stress-case.
   3. OGB-Arxiv-DGL  (169 k nodes, 1.1 M edges, 40 time-ordered splits) – 40-layer propagation stress-case.

C. DATA PRE-PROCESSING
   • All graphs are stored in pinned host RAM at start-up → no disk I/O during epochs.
   • For OGB datasets we follow ogb.nodeproppred.Dataset; Chameleon is loaded from pyg-data and converted to DGLGraph.
   • Feature normalisation – per feature channel z-score (µ,σ) computed on training split only; mean/σ cached.
   • Add explicit self-loop to every node before training.
   • Feature compression – float32 kept (A100 has bandwidth, avoids quantisation noise).
   • For Arxiv we create year-based OOD splits (≤2014 train, 2015–16 val, 2017–19 test) in addition to the official split for robustness (§H).

D. DATA SPLITTING
   • OGB official boolean masks for Products & Arxiv.
   • Chameleon – 10 random stratified splits (75 / 10 / 15).  We publish index files for reproducibility.
   • For robustness study (§H-2) we additionally create feature-noise and structural-perturbation versions (same split masks).

E. TRAIN / VAL / TEST PROTOCOL
   • Optimiser AdamW(lr=3e-4, wd=1e-2).  Cosine decay w/ warmup 5 epochs.
   • Batch size = 1024 root nodes for Products, 4096 for Arxiv, full-batch for Chameleon.
   • Epoch limit 500; early-stop if validation accuracy has not improved ≥0.1 % for 30 epochs.
   • Three seeds {0,1,2}.  We report mean±std over seeds; *Best-Val* model along training trajectory is restored and evaluated on the test mask.

F. SAMPLERS (COMPARISONS)
   S0  Full-batch (no sampling)
   S1  GraphSAINT-Edge (Chen ’20) – p=0.001 for Products, p=0.01 for Arxiv (tuned for ~same GPU RAM)
   S2  Bandit-BS.M  (Zhao ’21)
   S3  LBS          (Zhang ’22)
   S4  BLABS        (Xu ’23)
   S5  HYPE-BABS    (ours) – α=0.5, β=0.02, classes {1,2,4,8,16}, global K₀∈{64,128,256}.  Kmin = max(16,0.1·K₀).
   *All baseline hyper-parameters are taken verbatim from the original repos; only batch-size is adapted to fit A100 VRAM.*¹

G. EVALUATION METRICS
   Primary
     • Test Accuracy (%)  – Products/Arxiv (micro-F1), Chameleon (macro-F1 due to class-imbalance)
     • Mean Epoch Latency Δt (seconds) – arithmetic mean over *all* training epochs (excluding evaluation)
     • Mean Epoch Energy Δe (kJ)       – power integral from NVIDIA-SMI every 100 ms → Simpson rule.
   Secondary
     • Area under Pareto Front (AUPF_t, AUPF_e) – higher is better
     • GPU Memory peak (GB) – torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated()
     • Average #Sampled Edges per root batch
     • Effective gradient variance (σ² / σ²_full-batch) – estimated by Hutchinson trace
     • Calibration ECE (%) on test set

H. ROBUSTNESS & HYPER-PARAM STUDY
   H-1  Hyper-parameters (run on Products only)
        • α ∈ {0.0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0}
        • β ∈ {0.0,0.01,0.02,0.05}
        • Global K₀ ∈ {32,64,128,256,512}
        Grid = 5×4×5; Latin-Hypercube subsample 24 points; two seeds each.
        Metric: AUPF; we fit a surrogate GP and produce Sobol sensitivity scores.
   H-2  Robustness
        a) Feature noise: add Gaussian N(0,0.1·σ_feat) to 20 % random nodes at inference.
        b) Structural noise: drop 2 % random edges, re-evaluate test set.
        c) OOD split (Arxiv time split described above).
        d) adversarial perturb. – FGSM (ϵ=0.05) on node features for 500 random test nodes.
        We report ΔAccuracy vs clean.

I. COMPUTE & RESOURCE ACCOUNTING
   • FLOPs – torchprofile + custom hook for sparse matmuls; we log per epoch and average.
   • Latency & Energy already described.
   • Monetary cost @$1.58/hr (A100 spot, us-west) – runtime×rate.
