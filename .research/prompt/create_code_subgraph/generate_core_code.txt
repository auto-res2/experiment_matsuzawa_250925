# Experimental Design
- Strategy: --------------------------------------------------
Experiment 1.  Budget–Pareto Evaluation on Static Benchmarks
--------------------------------------------------
Goal
    Quantify the joint improvement in accuracy, variance and energy that TADP-HABLS delivers, and verify that the unified Lagrangian really finds the best trade-off under tight GPU-mem / energy caps.

Protocol
1. Datasets & models
      ogbn-products (2-layer GAT)         – medium, homophilic
      ogbn-papers100M (4-layer GATv2)     – very large, homophilic
      Friendster (8-layer Graph-Transformer) – billion-edge social graph
   All runs on a single A100 (80 GB).
2. Budgets
      a) Memory caps: 6 GB, 10 GB, 14 GB
      b) Energy caps: 0.3 kWh, 0.6 kWh
3. Methods
      • TADP-HABLS (full)
      • –M1 (no SW-DPP)  –M2 (no look-ahead)  –M4 (no dual tuner)  –M5 (no full-system meter)
      • HABLS, DPA-HABLS, LABOR-*, NS(25)
4. Metrics (per Table 1 in the paper)
      Test Acc / AUROC, neighbour-variance, inter-batch Jaccard, JEPE, JEPTA, wall-clock.
5. 5 random seeds, Adam 0.005, early-stop=50.

What Positive Results Would Show
• TADP-HABLS dominates the Pareto frontier: for every baseline point at (accuracy, JEPE) there exists a setting of λ₁-λ₃ that is ≥ 0.5 % more accurate while consuming ≥ 30 % less energy.
• Removing SW-DPP increases inter-batch Jaccard from ≈0.18→0.40 and drops accuracy by ≈1 %; removing the dual tuner forces manual fan-outs to recover similar accuracy but at 15–25 % higher JEPE — proving each component’s contribution.
• Under the 6 GB cap the method still holds 95 % of its large-memory accuracy, validating the adaptive fan-out.

Ultimate Demonstration
Shows that the unified objective and temporal diversity give simultaneous gains in all three target dimensions (variance, redundancy, energy) without manual tuning.

Experiment 2.  Streaming Graph Setting – Adaptivity Over Time
--------------------------------------------------
Goal
    Verify that (i) the sliding-window DPP keeps exploration high over long horizons and (ii) the GRU look-ahead removes the one-iteration attention lag on rapidly evolving graphs.

Protocol
1. Dynamic datasets
      Reddit-Temporal   (minute-level post graph, 50 M edges/month)
      Yelp-Timeline     (business review graph, 8 M edges/month)
   3-layer GeniePath encoder.
2. 4×A100 DGX box: one worker/GPU sampling its own mini-batches; gradients aggregated with DDP.
3. Compare three samplers
      a) TADP-HABLS (W = 12 epochs, GRU-look-ahead)
      b) HABLS + time-agnostic DPP (per-batch only)
      c) GraphSAINT-rw (standard random-walk)
4. Run 100 k chronological micro-batches (≈48 h wall-clock).
5. Record every 250 batches
      • Inter-GPU overlap (Jaccard)
      • Coverage ratio of new high-attention vertices
      • Streaming test accuracy vs wall-clock
      • Extra forward-pass latency (ms) per batch
      • JEPE.

What Positive Results Would Show
• TADP-HABLS maintains neighbour overlap <0.2 across the whole 48 h while HABLS drifts to >0.45 after 12 h, confirming sustained temporal diversity.
• Coverage of unseen high-attention nodes stays within the 1–1/e theoretical bound (>63 %) for W = 12, matching the paper claim.
• GRU predictor removes ≈22 % of the added forward-pass latency relative to a naive “use last α”, keeping throughput within 2 % of GraphSAINT.
• Streaming accuracy curve for TADP-HABLS rises 1.3 % faster within the first 15 k batches and ends 0.8 % higher, proving that faster attention feedback matters in non-stationary graphs.
• Energy per processed edge is 35 % lower than GraphSAINT, 18 % lower than HABLS, showing benefit of the energy term even in distributed training.

Ultimate Demonstration
Confirms that the method’s temporal modules (SW-DPP, GRU predictor) solve the exploration-exploitation problem in dynamic settings without sacrificing speed or energy.

--------------------------------------------------
Experiment 3.  Scalability & Kernel-Compression Micro-benchmarks
--------------------------------------------------
Goal
    Stress-test the compressed-embedding kernel (M3) at billion-node scale and verify that sampler cost remains sub-linear in graph size.

Protocol
1. Synthetic power-law graph generator: 1 B nodes, 100 B edges, stored on local NVMe RAID; embeddings pre-initialised (randn, 768-d).
2. Sampler micro-loop executed on single A100, varying reservoir size K ∈ {10 k, 50 k, 100 k} and sketch dimension d′ ∈ {128,256,512}.
3. Compare
      a) Full cosine kernel DPP (reference, fits only up to 50 k nodes)
      b) Nyström + exact cosine (HABLS)
      c) Our CountSketch + RFF compressed kernel (TADP-HABLS)
4. Measurements
      • Kernel construction time / batch
      • Peak GPU memory
      • Frobenius error ||K̂ − K||_F / ||K||_F
      • Downstream acceptance rate of DPP sampler (proxy for quality).

What Positive Results Would Show
• At K = 100 k, compressed kernel builds in <40 ms (8 × faster than HABLS) and fits in 1.1 GB GPU RAM (16 × smaller than reference) while keeping relative error <2 %.
• Acceptance rate of the k-DPP within 0.5 % of the exact kernel, indicating negligible impact on diversity quality.
• Empirically, kernel build time grows roughly O(K·log d′) instead of O(K²), confirming the claimed O(K d²) complexity.

Ultimate Demonstration
Proves that the kernel-compression trick is the key enabler for bringing DPP-style diversity into the billion-node regime on a single 80 GB GPU.
