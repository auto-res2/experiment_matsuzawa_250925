# Experimental Design
- Strategy: ────────────────────────────────
Experiment 1: Large-scale Accuracy–Efficiency Benchmark
────────────────────────────────
Goal
• Show that HAQ-GAT occupies a superior Pareto point (accuracy vs speed/memory/energy) compared with dense and existing sparse GAT variants.

Protocol
1. Datasets: MAG240M-S (1.9 B edges), OGB-Products, Reddit.
2. Models: HAQ-GAT, Dense GAT, SLiKC-GAT, KS-GAT, Sparse Top-K, INT8 Degree-Quant GAT.
3. Hardware: single A100-80 GB for training; inference also timed on Jetson-Orin NX.
4. Train 3 runs/seed, identical optimiser & schedule.
5. Log every epoch: val acc, wall-clock, GPU-MEM, nvidia-smi energy draw.
6. For each inference checkpoint record:       
   – latency & throughput (A100 + Orin)     
   – peak RAM/VRAM     
   – CodeCarbon CO₂.

Expected Demonstration
• HAQ-GAT matches dense GAT within ±0.2 % accuracy while being ≥4× faster and ≥5× lighter on MAG240M.
• INT8 path keeps ≤0.3 % accuracy loss but cuts Orin energy ≥60 % and RAM ≥3.5× vs FP32 SLiKC.
• Pareto front plot should place HAQ-GAT strictly top-right (best accuracy, lowest cost).

────────────────────────────────
Experiment 2: Online Error-Budget & Adaptive Sparsity Study
────────────────────────────────
Goal
• Verify that the error-budget controller keeps ‖Â−A‖∞ below user-supplied ε and that K₀ & m adapt to data dynamics with minimal overhead.

Protocol
1. Use OGB-Arxiv plus a synthetic, time-evolving graph where edge weights drift every 10 epochs.
2. Run HAQ-GAT under ε∈{1e-2,5e-3,1e-3}.  Track per-epoch:          
   – empirical ‖Â−A‖∞ and Jackknife CI     
   – K₀, m, exact-edge ratio     
   – ΔL validation loss when K₀ is changed.
3. Comparison: same model but with fixed Top-K as in SLiKC (matched initial K).
4. Report violation rate (% epochs where error>ε) and cumulative FLOPs.

Expected Demonstration
• HAQ-GAT keeps CI upper-bound ≤ε in >97 % epochs for all ε values; SLiKC violates >35 % at ε≤5e-3.
• When graph drifts, K₀ and m rise automatically, then decay once stable—showing adaptability with ≤8 % extra FLOPs compared to fixed-K baseline that must be over-provisioned by 2× to achieve same bound.
• Validation loss ΔL curves flatten exactly at γ threshold, confirming stopping rule.

────────────────────────────────
Experiment 3: Fairness & Quantisation Robustness on Degree-Imbalanced Graphs
────────────────────────────────
Goal
• Show that the built-in fairness guard alleviates degree bias and that INT8 training/inference remain robust on heterophilous, degree-skewed graphs.

Protocol
1. Datasets: Pokec-Zak (privacy, high skew), Texas & Wisconsin (heterophily), plus a generated power-law graph (α=2.5).
2. Train three variants:      
   a) HAQ-GAT (full)      
   b) HAQ-GAT w/o fairness guard      
   c) SLiKC-GAT.
3. Metrics per degree quintile: accuracy/F1, average # precise edges, and discard-rate gap.  Fairness metric = |Acc_lowQ – Acc_highQ|.
4. Run each variant in INT8 and FP32; compare accuracy drop and gradient stability (κ-value of second-order gradients).

Expected Demonstration
• Full HAQ-GAT reduces fairness gap to ≤1.5 % (vs 8 % SLiKC; 5 % without guard) without hurting macro accuracy.
• Precise-edge allocation histogram shows re-balancing toward low-degree nodes after guard kicks in.
• INT8 version preserves ≥99 % of FP32 accuracy across all graphs; κ indicates no gradient explosion/vanishing, proving QAT path is numerically stable.

────────────────────────────────
Combined Outcome
Together the three experiments exhibit: (1) superior real-world efficiency, (2) provable adaptive control of approximation error, and (3) responsible behaviour w.r.t. fairness and low-precision deployment—validating the holistic effectiveness of HAQ-GAT.
