# Experimental Design

## Experiment Strategy
────────────────────────────────
Experiment 1: Large-scale Accuracy–Efficiency Benchmark
────────────────────────────────
Goal
• Show that HAQ-GAT occupies a superior Pareto point (accuracy vs speed/memory/energy) compared with dense and existing sparse GAT variants.

Protocol
1. Datasets: MAG240M-S (1.9 B edges), OGB-Products, Reddit.
2. Models: HAQ-GAT, Dense GAT, SLiKC-GAT, KS-GAT, Sparse Top-K, INT8 Degree-Quant GAT.
3. Hardware: single A100-80 GB for training; inference also timed on Jetson-Orin NX.
4. Train 3 runs/seed, identical optimiser & schedule.
5. Log every epoch: val acc, wall-clock, GPU-MEM, nvidia-smi energy draw.
6. For each inference checkpoint record:       
   – latency & throughput (A100 + Orin)     
   – peak RAM/VRAM     
   – CodeCarbon CO₂.

Expected Demonstration
• HAQ-GAT matches dense GAT within ±0.2 % accuracy while being ≥4× faster and ≥5× lighter on MAG240M.
• INT8 path keeps ≤0.3 % accuracy loss but cuts Orin energy ≥60 % and RAM ≥3.5× vs FP32 SLiKC.
• Pareto front plot should place HAQ-GAT strictly top-right (best accuracy, lowest cost).

────────────────────────────────
Experiment 2: Online Error-Budget & Adaptive Sparsity Study
────────────────────────────────
Goal
• Verify that the error-budget controller keeps ‖Â−A‖∞ below user-supplied ε and that K₀ & m adapt to data dynamics with minimal overhead.

Protocol
1. Use OGB-Arxiv plus a synthetic, time-evolving graph where edge weights drift every 10 epochs.
2. Run HAQ-GAT under ε∈{1e-2,5e-3,1e-3}.  Track per-epoch:          
   – empirical ‖Â−A‖∞ and Jackknife CI     
   – K₀, m, exact-edge ratio     
   – ΔL validation loss when K₀ is changed.
3. Comparison: same model but with fixed Top-K as in SLiKC (matched initial K).
4. Report violation rate (% epochs where error>ε) and cumulative FLOPs.

Expected Demonstration
• HAQ-GAT keeps CI upper-bound ≤ε in >97 % epochs for all ε values; SLiKC violates >35 % at ε≤5e-3.
• When graph drifts, K₀ and m rise automatically, then decay once stable—showing adaptability with ≤8 % extra FLOPs compared to fixed-K baseline that must be over-provisioned by 2× to achieve same bound.
• Validation loss ΔL curves flatten exactly at γ threshold, confirming stopping rule.

────────────────────────────────
Experiment 3: Fairness & Quantisation Robustness on Degree-Imbalanced Graphs
────────────────────────────────
Goal
• Show that the built-in fairness guard alleviates degree bias and that INT8 training/inference remain robust on heterophilous, degree-skewed graphs.

Protocol
1. Datasets: Pokec-Zak (privacy, high skew), Texas & Wisconsin (heterophily), plus a generated power-law graph (α=2.5).
2. Train three variants:      
   a) HAQ-GAT (full)      
   b) HAQ-GAT w/o fairness guard      
   c) SLiKC-GAT.
3. Metrics per degree quintile: accuracy/F1, average # precise edges, and discard-rate gap.  Fairness metric = |Acc_lowQ – Acc_highQ|.
4. Run each variant in INT8 and FP32; compare accuracy drop and gradient stability (κ-value of second-order gradients).

Expected Demonstration
• Full HAQ-GAT reduces fairness gap to ≤1.5 % (vs 8 % SLiKC; 5 % without guard) without hurting macro accuracy.
• Precise-edge allocation histogram shows re-balancing toward low-degree nodes after guard kicks in.
• INT8 version preserves ≥99 % of FP32 accuracy across all graphs; κ indicates no gradient explosion/vanishing, proving QAT path is numerically stable.

────────────────────────────────
Combined Outcome
Together the three experiments exhibit: (1) superior real-world efficiency, (2) provable adaptive control of approximation error, and (3) responsible behaviour w.r.t. fairness and low-precision deployment—validating the holistic effectiveness of HAQ-GAT.

EXPERIMENT 1 – LARGE-SCALE ACCURACY/EFFICIENCY BENCHMARK
────────────────────────────────────────────
Goal
    Quantify the Pareto-front (accuracy-vs-cost) of HAQ-GAT compared with dense and existing sparse GAT variants on billion-edge scale graphs.

1. Models
    Main:   • HAQ-GAT (ours, full stack)
    Baselines:
        – Dense GAT (PyG implementation, 8 heads, hidden=256)*
        – SLiKC-GAT†
        – KS-GAT (kernel-sparsity, 2023)†
        – Sparse Top-K GAT (K=20) – matches GraphSAINT top-k routine
        – INT8 Degree-Quant GAT (vector-wise int8 quantisation, no sparsity)
    *All baselines re-implemented in PyTorch 2.1 + PyG 2.5 for identical kernels.
    †Official authors’ hyper-parameters were re-tuned on validation split using the same search space listed below.

2. Datasets
    • MAG240M-S (OGB, 1.9 B edges, 244 M nodes, 768-d feats)
    • OGB-Products (2.4 M nodes, 61 M edges, 100-d feats)
    • Reddit (232 K nodes, 11 M edges, 602-d feats)

3. Dataset Pre-processing
    a. Feature normalisation: per-feature z-score.
    b. Dimensionality squeeze (MAG240M only): random-projection to 256-d (orthogonal matrix, seed-sync across models) to fit into A100 memory while keeping information.*
    c. Add self-loops, coalesce duplicate edges.
    *Dense GAT requires this to fit 80 GB; we apply the same projection to every model to keep things fair.

4. Data Splits
    • Use official OGB train/valid/test node indices.
    • Reddit: 60/20/20 random split, stratified by label, identical across seeds.

5. Optimiser & Schedule (shared)
    – AdamW, base-LR∈{1e-3,2e-3,5e-4}, weight-decay∈{0,1e-4,5e-4}, dropout∈{0,0.2,0.4}.  20-trial Optuna search per model+dataset.
    – Cosine with 5-epoch warm-up, 200 total epochs (MAG240M 40), early-stop patience 30.
    – Three seeds {1,2,3} ⇒ 3× trials retained per model.
    – Check-pointing best-val-acc.

6. Primary & Secondary Metrics
    Primary:   • Node classification Accuracy (Micro) on test.
    Secondary: • Macro-F1, • Peak GPU-MEM (MB), • Wall-clock train-time/epoch (s), • Total train-energy (kJ via nvidia-smi –q –d POWER), • Inference latency (ms/node) and throughput (kNodes/s) on A100 & Jetson Orin NX (INT8 + FP32 paths), • CodeCarbon CO₂-eq (g), • Theoretical and empirical FLOPs.

7. Repetitions & Aggregation
    – Report mean±std over seeds.  For each seed the best-val checkpoint is evaluated on test and efficiency measured on that frozen model.

8. Robustness Add-on (covers all experiments once)
    – Add Gaussian noise σ∈{0.01,0.05} to 10 % of node features at inference; measure ΔAcc.

9. Hyper-parameter Sensitivity (HAQ-GAT only)
    – One-at-a-time sweep: {γ∈[10⁻⁴ … 10⁻²], ε∈{1e-2,5e-3,1e-3}, K₀_init∈{4,8,16}} on OGB-Products.
    – Plot surface ΔAcc vs FLOPs.

10. Resource Profiling
    – Use PyTorch-Profiler to record CUDA kernel time, forward/backward memory.
    – ptflops to compute theoretical FLOPs.  CUDA events for wall-clock.

Goal
    Validate that HAQ-GAT respects user error bound ε while adapting K₀ & m under temporal graph drift.

1. Data
    • OGB-Arxiv (time-stamped).  Augment with synthetic drift: every 10 epochs add ±5 % random perturbation to 20 % edge weights (drawn from N(0,0.1)).

2. Models & Variants
    a) HAQ-GAT with controller, ε∈{1e-2,5e-3,1e-3}
    b) Fixed-K SLiKC-GAT (K set so that initial ∥Â−A∥∞≈ε)

3. Split & Training
    – Chronological split: 1991-2008 train, 2009-2011 val, 2012-2013 test.
    – 5 seeds.

4. Logged Quantities per Epoch
    • ∥Â−A∥∞ (empirical), Jackknife 95 % CI, K₀, m, exact-edge ratio, validation loss, cumulative FLOPs.

5. Violation Metric
    – Violation rate = |{epochs: upper-CI>ε}| / total.

6. Controller Overhead
    – Extra FLOPs (%) vs baseline per epoch.

7. Evaluation & Visualisation
    – Plot K₀ & m over epochs showing spikes when drift injected.
    – Heat-map ε vs violation-rate vs FLOPs.

EXPERIMENT 3 – FAIRNESS & QUANTISATION ROBUSTNESS
────────────────────────────────────────────
Goal
    Demonstrate mitigation of degree bias and stability of INT8 training/inference.

1. Data Sets
    • Pokec-Zak (347 K nodes, 30 M edges, privacy-sensitive labels)
    • Texas (183 nodes), Wisconsin (251) – heterophilous WebKB
    • Synthetic power-law (α=2.5, 100 K nodes, 2 M edges) generated with NetworkX powerlaw_cluster_graph.

2. Models
    a) HAQ-GAT (full)
    b) HAQ-GAT – Fairness-Guard OFF
    c) SLiKC-GAT

3. Training Details
    – Same optimiser grid as Exp-1.  Seeds {1,2,3,4,5} (small graphs are cheap).
    – INT8 QAT: quant-aware training for last 30 % epochs, then export INT8.

4. Metrics
    Primary:   • Node accuracy/F1.
    Fairness:  • Degree-gap = |Acc_low-quintile – Acc_high-quintile|.
    Secondary: • # precise edges per node quintile, • Discard-rate gap, • κ-value (ratio of largest to smallest non-zero eigen-values of Hessian; stability proxy).
    Quant-robustness: • ΔAcc_FP32→INT8 (%), • Gradient-scale ratio (max/min) across layers.

5. Robustness Stress
    – Feature noise σ=0.05, structure noise: randomly delete 5 % edges of high-degree nodes only, test repeat.

6. Early-Stopping
    – Patience 100; report last epoch (small graphs converge quickly).

7. Example Metric Extraction
    ```python
    def degree_quintile_mask(deg):
        q = torch.quantile(deg.float(), torch.arange(0,1.1,0.2))
        return [ (deg>=q[i]) & (deg<q[i+1]) for i in range(5) ]
    ```
