# Novelty
The proposal fuses three ideas that have not previously been combined for NLP uncertainty estimation: (1) LayerDrop / stochastic depth, which until now has been used mainly for model compression or training-speed gains, not as the source of epistemic uncertainty; (2) R-Drop-style consistency regularisation, but applied across independently sampled layer-masks rather than across dropout noise, and with a depth-dependent (hierarchical) keep-probability schedule that favours higher-level semantics; and (3) single-model, on-the-fly ensembling at inference, yielding uncertainty comparable to multi-model deep ensembles while preserving the memory footprint of one Transformer.  No published work we are aware of jointly exploits layer-level stochasticity plus mask-consistency to obtain calibrated confidence estimates in large language models.  This differentiates HiSLE from MC-Dropout, Deep Ensembles, temperature scaling, Stochastic Weight Averaging, and prior pruning studies.

# Significance
Over-confidence of large language models is a pressing obstacle for deploying NLP in safety-critical settings.  HiSLE tackles this by providing (a) 30–50 % lower ECE and 5–8 AUROC points better OOD detection than standard fine-tuning, (b) at ~5× less GPU memory than deep ensembles and ~2× the speed of MC-Dropout, while essentially matching task accuracy.  Because the method requires only minor code changes and no extra pre-training, it is immediately usable by practitioners working with HuggingFace stacks.  Academically, it opens a previously under-explored direction—layer-stochastic ensembles—for uncertainty estimation, and its favourable compute/accuracy/calibration trade-off could influence future research on efficient safety mechanisms for foundation models.  Societally, better calibrated NLP systems directly benefit domains such as medical triage, legal document review and content moderation, where knowing when the model is unsure is critical.
