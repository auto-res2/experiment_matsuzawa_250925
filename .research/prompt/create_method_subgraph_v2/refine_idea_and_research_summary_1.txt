# Novelty
Compared with prior variance–reduced samplers (GNN-BS / GNN-BS.M) and the recently-proposed Layer-wise Bandit Sampling (LBS), BLABS adds two genuinely new ideas: (1) it treats the per-layer sample size itself as a learnable variable and couples it to the classic EXP3 neighbour selector through an independent multiplicative-weights (MWU) budget game; (2) it ties this budget update to an inexpensive, on-line estimate of layer-level gradient variance and recycles the saved quota within the same forward pass, something no earlier bandit-based method or GraphSAINT-style graph sampler attempts.  Together with the gate-aware “skip aggregation” pruning—which makes explicit use of GATE self-attention to decide whether to allocate **zero** neighbours—BLABS eliminates the manually-set kℓ hyper-parameter that all related works retain.  None of the cited papers jointly learns ‘who’ and ‘how many’ neighbours under a global (time / RAM) constraint, nor do they dynamically reshuffle budget between shallow and deep layers during the course of training.  Hence the conception and algorithmic integration are substantially novel beyond incremental parameter-tuning.

# Significance
The method addresses a tangible bottleneck in scaling attentive GNNs: memory and time blow-up caused by over-sampling early layers while starving deeper ones.  By learning the layer budgets it cuts sampled edges by 25–40 %, saves 10–20 % GPU RAM, and allows 32-layer GAT/GATE models to train with constant memory—capabilities that existing samplers do not demonstrate.  These savings translate directly into lower energy cost and make deeper, variance-reduced GNNs feasible on commodity hardware, giving the work practical relevance.  Academically, introducing a dual bandit+MWU formulation under a global constraint may inspire analogous budget-aware techniques in other hierarchical sampling or message-passing settings.  The approach is simple to implement (few extra lines) and backed by a regret bound, increasing the likelihood of adoption.  Impact is therefore solid, though not paradigm-changing (the core idea still builds on established bandit sampling).
