# Paper Outline
ProtoDiff builds upon prototypical networks and diffusion models. It involves a three-step process: 1) Per-task prototype overfitting: A meta-learner is initially trained, then fine-tuned on individual tasks to obtain 'overfitted prototypes' (zi,*) by minimizing cross-entropy loss, serving as ground truth. 2) Task-guided diffusion: A generative process is meta-learned to transition prototypes from a vanilla form (˜z) to the overfitted state. During meta-training, the diffusion model (ϵθ, implemented as a GPT-2 based transformer) is parameterized to predict the prototype (signal prediction) and trained using a combined objective of diffusion loss (Ldiff) and cross-entropy loss (LCE). In the meta-test stage, task-specific prototypes are generated by gradually denoising random noise (zT) conditioned on the vanilla prototype (˜z) derived from support samples. 3) Residual prototype learning: Instead of predicting the overfitted prototype directly, the model predicts the residual (z∗ − ˜z), leveraging its sparsity. This accelerates training and improves performance, especially when combined with identity initialization.
