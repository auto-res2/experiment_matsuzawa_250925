# Experiment Strategy

Title: One-Shot Pareto Benchmark of HYPE-BABS on a Single NVIDIA A100

Goal
Quantitatively prove that HYPE-BABS delivers a uniformly better Accuracy–Latency–Energy trade-off than state-of-the-art neighbour samplers on realistic, large-scale GNN training.  

Rationale
• The A100 has ample memory (80 GB) to host all candidate samplers simultaneously, eliminating I/O variance.  
• A single, carefully controlled hardware setting isolates the sampler’s contribution from inter-device noise.  
• A unified Pareto evaluation condenses three objectives into one visual, decisive verdict.  

Experimental Steps
1. Datasets (hosted in RAM → zero loading jitter)
   a. OGB-Products (large, homophilic)
   b. Chameleon (medium, heterophilic)
   c. OGB-Arxiv-DGL (40-layer stress test)

2. Model
   16-layer GATv2, hidden dim 128, ELU, dropout 0.2.  Identical initialisation across runs (torch.manual_seed 42).  

3. Samplers
   Full-batch, GraphSAINT-Edge, Bandit-BS.M, LBS, BLABS, HYPE-BABS (ours).  Hyper-parameters copied verbatim from their papers; HYPE-BABS uses default α = 0.5, β = 0.02, K₀ ∈ {64,128,256}.  

4. Measurement pipeline
   • Wrap the training loop in a Python context manager that every 100 ms records:
     – Wall-clock time (time.perf_counter).
     – Instantaneous board power draw via nvidia-smi --query-gpu=power.draw.
   • Integrate power over time to obtain energy (Δe).  
   • Log train / validation loss, accuracy, global budget K_t, and per-layer variance.

5. Training protocol
   • 500 epochs, AdamW (lr 3e-4, weight-decay 1e-2).  
   • Early stop if validation accuracy saturates for 30 epochs.  
   • Three seeds per setting to report mean±σ.  

6. Evaluation metrics
   a. Final test accuracy.
   b. Mean epoch latency (Δt).
   c. Mean epoch energy (Δe).
   d. Area under Pareto Front (AUPF): integrate convex hull in (accuracy, latency) and (accuracy, energy) planes.  Larger = better.

7. Analysis
   • Plot Pareto fronts of all samplers.  HYPE-BABS must lie strictly on the upper-left (better) boundary.  
   • Overlay budget-annealing curves K_t to visualise adaptive shrinking.  
   • Histogram node-wise k after epoch 400 to show heavy-tailed distribution.  
   • Ablations (α = 1, no annealing, one-level bandit) run only on OGB-Products to isolate each component’s impact.  

Expected Outcomes and What They Demonstrate
1. HYPE-BABS reaches within ±0.2 % of the highest accuracy among all samplers while reducing median epoch time by ≈35 % and energy by ≈40 % compared with BLABS.  ⇒ Confirms P1 & P2: fine-grained, latency-aware budgeting lowers hardware cost without hurting performance.
2. Its Pareto front dominates: AUPF improves by ≥15 % over the second-best method on every dataset.  ⇒ Shows multi-objective optimality, not just one metric.
3. Annealing curve: global edge budget K_t falls to <25 % of K₀ after 300 epochs, yet accuracy plateaus, unlike baselines whose K stays flat.  ⇒ Verifies P3: adaptive budget shrinkage removes wasted computation.
4. Node-wise k histogram: 70 % of low-degree nodes end with k = 1, while top 5 % hubs retain k ≥ 8.  Variance heat-map confirms smoother per-layer gradients.  ⇒ Validates node×layer granularity (addresses P1).
5. Ablations: removing latency term raises energy by ~25 %; disabling annealing increases edges processed by ~18 % with no accuracy gain; discarding budget-bandit drops accuracy −2 %.  ⇒ Each design choice is necessary.

Conclusion
Executing this single, tightly controlled A100 experiment yields decisive evidence that HYPE-BABS simultaneously minimises training time and energy for a given accuracy, fulfilling the three open problems left by BLABS.  The strategy leverages the powerful hardware to eliminate confounders and presents results in an industry-relevant Pareto framework that stakeholders can immediately interpret.
